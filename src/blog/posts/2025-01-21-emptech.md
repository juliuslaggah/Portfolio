On the afternoon of 21 January 2025, after our morning at [Hytera](/blog/hytera/), we visited
**Shenzhen Emperor Technology Company Limited**, better known as **Emptech**, in Shenzhen's Nanshan District.
Emptech describes itself as a **"trusted identity products and solutions provider"**.

## About Emptech

Emptech was founded in **1995** and listed on the Shenzhen Stock Exchange in **2016**. It builds the technology
behind secure identity: systems that **capture people's biometric data**, **produce and personalise ID
documents** (such as e-passports and national ID cards), **issue** them and **verify** them later. Its
showroom displays describe it as a supplier of e-passport personalisation equipment in China, and of identity
and voter systems for several countries in Africa and Asia.

## Connecting to West Africa: Nigeria's elections

What caught my attention most was Emptech's work in Nigeria. The company has worked with Nigeria's
**Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC)** for more than a decade:

- From **2012**, it supported the production of **voter cards** for tens of millions of Nigerian voters.
- For the **2023 general elections**, it manufactured the **Bimodal Voter Accreditation System (BVAS)**
  devices. These are handheld tablets that check a voter's identity using **fingerprint and facial
  recognition** before they cast their ballot, and then help upload polling-unit results.

BVAS is not online voting: Nigerians still vote on paper. But using biometrics to verify voters and digital
tools to transmit results is one of the largest uses of election technology in Africa.

## Smart, secure everyday technology

Emptech's showroom also featured devices for verifying documents and **smart registration plates** with QR
codes for bicycles and electric vehicles, which help cities register and track vehicles.

## What I took away

- **Identity is the foundation of digital services.** Banking, healthcare, social support and voting all
  depend on knowing who someone is, securely and fairly.
- **Elections and trust.** Technology can make elections faster and harder to manipulate, but only if people
  trust it. That requires transparency, good governance and independent oversight. This connects directly to
  my research on trustworthy technology and user trust.
- **Lessons for Sierra Leone.** As Sierra Leone develops its own digital identity and electoral systems,
  examples from Nigeria and other countries offer valuable lessons on what works and what to watch out for.
