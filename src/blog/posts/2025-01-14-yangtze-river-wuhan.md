After the Hubei Provincial Museum, we ended the day with a casual visit to the **Yangtze River**. At about
6,300 km, it is the longest river in China and in all of Asia, and the third-longest in the world. Wuhan
grew up where the Yangtze meets its largest tributary, the Han River, which splits the city into its three
historic towns: Wuchang, Hankou and Hanyang.

## Under the First Bridge

We stood on the Wuchang side, right beneath the **Wuhan Yangtze River Bridge**, the first bridge ever
built across the Yangtze. It opened in **1957** and links Snake Hill in Wuchang with Turtle Hill in Hanyang.
It's a double-deck bridge: cars cross on the upper level and trains on the lower level. Across the water you
can see the TV tower on Turtle Hill rising through the haze.

## A moment to reflect

It was a quiet, misty afternoon, and the riverside was full of locals and visitors walking along the bank.
After two busy days of technology companies and ancient history, it was a good place to slow down and think
about how infrastructure shapes a city. Nearly 70 years after it opened, this one bridge still connects
people, trade and rail traffic across one of the world's great rivers.
