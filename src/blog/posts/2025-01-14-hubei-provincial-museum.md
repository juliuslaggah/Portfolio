The day after visiting Dameng and FiberHome, we went to the **Hubei Provincial Museum** in Wuhan, beside
East Lake. It is one of China's national first-grade museums, with a collection of more than 460,000 objects
covering thousands of years of Chinese history. Walking through it felt like stepping into ancient China.

## The bronze chime bells

The highlight for me was the famous set of bronze chime bells (*bianzhong*) of the **Marquis Yi of Zeng**,
unearthed in 1978 in Suixian, Hubei, from a tomb more than 2,400 years old. Hung in rows on a huge wooden
frame, it is the largest and best-preserved set of chime bells ever found in China. The bells can still
play music today, and seeing them in person was unforgettable.

## Bronzes, dragons and the Chu kingdom

Much of the collection comes from the ancient **Chu** kingdom and the tombs of the Zeng state. The
craftsmanship is extraordinary: bronzes covered in coiling dragons, lacquerware, jade and early writing on
bamboo slips. The museum also holds the **Sword of Goujian**, a bronze sword about 2,500 years old that is
still sharp and has barely rusted.

## What I took away

- **Engineering is ancient.** Making a set of bells that each play two distinct notes took precise
  knowledge of metallurgy, acoustics and mathematics more than two thousand years ago.
- **Preserving heritage is a data problem too.** Museums like this one use digitisation and careful
  records to protect history, a reminder of how technology can serve culture.
- **History gives context to innovation.** After two days of visiting high-tech companies, it was powerful to
  see how deep the roots of China's science and craftsmanship go.
