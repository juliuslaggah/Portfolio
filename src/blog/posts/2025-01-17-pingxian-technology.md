Our final visit on 17 January 2025 was an evening tour of **Pingxian Technology** (平显科技) in the north of
**Shenzhen**. The company works with **glass materials** and produces **touch screens and interactive
displays**, the kind of technology we use every day on phones, kiosks and classroom smart boards.

## Inside the production line

The tour took us onto the factory floor, where we saw how a sheet of glass becomes part of a working screen.

<figure>
  <img src="/assets/img/pingxian-clean-room.jpg" alt="A bright, clean production room with large glass-topped work tables and a big round pressure chamber at the far end">
  <figcaption>A clean production room, with large work tables and a big pressure chamber at the far end.</figcaption>
</figure>

Glass for displays has to be kept almost perfectly clean, because even a speck of dust can ruin a screen.
That is why this work happens in bright, sealed rooms where staff wear protective gowns.

<figure>
  <img src="/assets/img/pingxian-inspection-machine.jpg" alt="An automated inspection machine with a dark sample panel on a platform, safety light curtains, emergency stop buttons and a monitoring screen">
  <figcaption>An automated, camera-based inspection station with safety light curtains and emergency stops.</figcaption>
</figure>

One machine used rows of cameras and lights to inspect panels automatically, a good example of
**computer vision** being used for quality control on a real production line.

## Touch screens in action

At the end of the tour, the team showed their finished products: large interactive displays you can write
and draw on with a finger, alongside all-in-one touch screens running apps and video. Displays like these are
increasingly used in classrooms, meeting rooms and public information kiosks.

## What I took away

- **Every screen starts as a material.** Seeing glass processed, cleaned and inspected made me appreciate how
  much engineering sits behind the screens we take for granted.
- **AI on the factory floor.** Automated optical inspection is a practical, everyday use of computer vision,
  a topic I can bring straight into my teaching.
- **Technology for classrooms.** Interactive displays could make lessons more engaging in Sierra Leone too,
  if they are affordable, durable and supported locally.

This visit rounded off an unforgettable week of learning in Wuhan and Shenzhen, from databases and optical
fibre to drones, robots, smart cities and touch screens.
