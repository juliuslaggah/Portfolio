During our tour of the [Longhua Future City Exhibition Hall](/blog/longhua-future-city/), one section
stood out for me: the **precision instruments and equipment** area, where Shenzhen companies showcase
their hardware. Much of it came from **HARWAR** (哈瓦), a Shenzhen company that designs and builds
**industrial-grade drones**.

## About HARWAR

HARWAR International Aviation Technology (Shenzhen) Co., Ltd. was founded around 2014 and focuses on the
research and development of industrial unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs). Unlike the small camera drones
most people know, these are large multi-rotor aircraft built to carry heavy equipment for long periods.
The company lists uses such as **emergency rescue, firefighting, disaster response, medical aid,
power-grid and oil-pipeline inspection, mapping and environmental monitoring**.

## Robots that go where people can't

Next to the drones was a small tracked **inspection robot** inside a clear tube. Robots like this crawl
through pipes and tunnels with cameras and lights, checking for damage in places that are dangerous or
impossible for people to reach.

## What I took away

- **Hardware meets software.** These machines depend on embedded systems, sensors, computer vision and
  control software, the same building blocks I teach.
- **Inspection and safety at scale.** Drones and robots can inspect power lines, pipelines and disaster
  zones faster and more safely. That has clear value for infrastructure and emergency response in
  Sierra Leone.
- **Responsible technology.** Powerful dual-use tools raise real questions about safety, regulation and
  how they should be used, which connects to my interest in trustworthy and human-centred technology.
